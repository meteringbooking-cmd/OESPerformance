Rumours swirl that upcoming TOM302 Newcastle to Enfidha flight may be missing its left and right flange

Experts are unable to confirm or deny the whereabouts of the left and right flange on TOMjet flight TOM302, which is due to depart Newcastle International Airport at 15:55 this afternoon bound for Enfidha-Hammamet International Airport, Tunisia.

By Alistair Pennywhistle, BBC Aviation Correspondent  |  Published: Today, 09:47  |  Updated: 11 minutes ago

Unconfirmed reports emerging from aviation circles this morning suggest that TOMjet flight TOM302, currently sitting on the tarmac at Newcastle International and scheduled for a 15:55 departure, may be preparing to operate with an absent, misplaced, or otherwise unaccounted-for left and right flange.

The aircraft, due to carry holidaymakers in various states of pre-holiday excitement, is currently being prepped for its afternoon slot. Ground crew reportedly noticed something "a bit off" about the left and right side of the aircraft during routine walk-arounds, though witnesses disagree on what, exactly, constitutes a flange and whether the one in question was ever there to begin with.

"I'm not saying it definitely doesn't have a left and right flange. I'm also not saying it does. What I am saying is that in my 34 years of standing next to aircraft, I have never seen a flange quite so absent."

— Derek Partridge, Senior Apron Marshal, Newcastle International Airport

Passengers waiting to board TOM302 are understood to be largely unaware of the flange situation, most being occupied in the departure lounge, buying an overpriced Boots meal deal, or attempting to queue in the Group 1 boarding lane despite their ticket clearly stating Group 4.

What we know so far

  • TOM302 is due to depart Newcastle International Airport at 15:55 this afternoon bound for Enfidha-Hammamet International, Tunisia
  • Rumours of a missing left and right flange emerged during routine pre-flight ground checks
  • The right flange has been visually confirmed by baggage handlers and is reported to be "doing great"
  • TOMjet has declined to comment on the precise location of the left and right flange prior to takeoff
  • Aviation experts confirm flanges are, technically, a real thing
  • No stray flanges have been found in the Duty-Free area at time of publication

A spokesperson for TOMjet told the BBC: "We can confirm that our aircraft are maintained to the highest standards ahead of every departure, that all required components are in their designated positions, and that we will not be issuing a statement specifically about flanges at this time." The spokesperson then hung up immediately.

The Civil Aviation Authority said it was "monitoring the situation closely," which aviation reporters interpret as meaning someone has written "FLANGE?" on a Post-it note and stuck it to a monitor.

Expert Reaction

"A flange, in the broader sense, serves an important flanging function. Whether this particular flange is strictly load-bearing for a 15:55 departure is a question above my pay grade. Or anyone's, frankly."

Prof. Gerald Mumford, Chair of Aeronautical Ambiguity, Cranfield University

Members of the public have taken to social media with wildly varying responses. One user wrote "thoughts and prayers for the flange," while another — identifying themselves as an aerospace engineer — posted a 47-slide thread explaining why flying flangeless is fine, before deleting it without explanation.

Flight tracking websites show TOM302 currently stationary at its gate, which aviation analysts note is "exactly what a plane with a missing flange would do hours before its 15:55 takeoff, if it was fine, which it probably is."
