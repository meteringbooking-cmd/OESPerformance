☕ The Cafetière Survival Guide

A mandatory workplace safety document commissioned entirely because of one person. You know who you are. Your left hand definitely knows who you are.

📋 Official Incident Report — The Kitchen, Tuesday Morning

At approximately [unknown time, estimated "before she'd had any coffee, which is deeply ironic"], Ashleigh approached the cafetière with confidence. She gripped the plunger. She pushed. The cafetière, sensing an opportunity, did not plunge. Instead, it squirted — unleashing a high-velocity jet of near-boiling coffee sideways from the lid, directly onto Ashleigh's left hand. Ashleigh is okay. The cafetière feels no remorse. It has been returned to active service because apparently we are all fine with this. This document exists so that the next person the cafetière targets has absolutely no excuse.

⚠️ PHYSICS REMINDER: The cafetière does not care about you. It is a glass tube of pressurised boiling liquid with a loose lid and it has already proven it will use this. Do not give it a reason.
Step-by-step instructions (read all of them, not just the fun one)
1
Pre-heat the cafetière

Pour a splash of hot water into the empty glass, swirl, and tip it out. This prevents thermal shock — cold glass + boiling water = shattering glass + a whole new incident report. We only have so many pages in the accident book.

Yes, this feels unnecessary. So did the squirting. Do the step.
2
Coarse grounds. COARSE.

The grind must be coarse — like sea salt, like rough sand, like the texture of your manager's feedback. Fine grounds clog the filter, build pressure, and then that pressure needs somewhere to go. That somewhere is you. Fine grounds are the direct cause of The Squirt. We are calling it The Squirt now officially.

Ashleigh has approved the name The Squirt. She's being very good about all of this.
3
Water: hot, not actively aggressive

Wait 30 seconds after the kettle boils. ~94°C is ideal. Pour slowly and in a gentle circle over the grounds — like you're watering a very fragile plant that, if upset, will explode and burn your colleague. Use about 1 heaped tablespoon of coffee per 100ml of water.

Do not pour it like you're angry. The cafetière will take notes.
4
Stir. Lid on. Walk away.

One gentle stir. Plunger pulled fully up. Lid on. Then set a timer for 4 minutes and leave. Do not hover. Do not come back at 90 seconds to "just have a look." The coffee is fine. It does not need you. Give it its 4 minutes of peace, which is more than you gave it last time.

It's brewing. It's fine. Go away.
⬅ THIS IS THE ONE. READ THIS ONE.
5
THE PLUNGE — the step that started all of this

One hand flat on the lid. Slow, steady, gentle downward pressure. Like pressing a lift button. Like patting a sleeping dog. The second you feel resistance: STOP. Do not "push through it." Do not assume it'll be fine. Resistance = pressure = The Squirt = Ashleigh's hand = this entire document. If it resists: remove plunger, stir, try again softly. Or have tea. Tea never did this to anyone.

Ask yourself: is this coffee worth it? Ashleigh asked herself this too. She knows the answer now.
6
Pour it. Into a mug. On purpose.

Pour immediately after plunging — leaving it stews the coffee and turns it bitter, which is the least of your problems at this stage. Aim for the inside of the mug. The mug has a handle. The handle is so your hand stays away from the hot liquid. This is not a design quirk. Use it. Pour slowly. Do not miss the mug. The mug is right there.

🩹 What to do when (not if) something goes wrong

DOCool immediately under running water for 20 full minutes. Not 5. Not "until it feels okay." Twenty minutes. Put on a podcast. Time it. The cooling is the treatment.
DOCover loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy dressing once cooled. Do not wrap tightly. It is a burn, not a parcel.
DOGo to A&E if it's blistering, larger than a 50p coin, or looks like something that warrants a professional opinion rather than a Google Image Search.
DON'TApply butter, toothpaste, ice, yoghurt, sudocrem, or any other substance you found in a home remedies thread. They don't work. They cause infections. Your nan is wrong about this.
DON'TUse the steam from the cafetière to "clean" the wound. This has been considered. The answer is no. Absolutely not. What are you doing.
DON'TImmediately make another coffee. You have just been burned by a coffee machine. Sit down. Have water. Fill in the accident book. Consider your relationship with caffeine.

☕ How to never be Ashleigh (prevention tips)

🔍Check the rubber seal on the plunger before every use. If the gasket is cracked, warped, shrunken, or inexplicably gone — do not use the cafetière. The gasket is the only thing preventing The Squirt. Without it you are just pushing a piston into hot liquid and hoping.
📏Don't fill it to the brim. Leave 2–3cm at the top. Overfilling means pressure has nowhere to go except sideways, which is where hands typically are. Give it room. Give yourself a margin of safety.
🏳️If it resists, you have already lost. The cafetière has decided. Accept this. Stir and retry gently, or make a different drink. Today is a tea day and that is okay. Herbal teas have never hospitalised anyone in this office.
🧼Clean it after every single use. Old grounds block the filter. Blocked filter = pressure = you know what pressure leads to. Rinse it. It takes 30 seconds. It prevents The Squirt. It is the bare minimum we ask of you.
🗒️If something seems wrong, write a note and stick it on. Stiff plunger? Cracked glass? Wobbly lid? Tell someone. Don't leave it for the next person to discover the hard way. We have a next person. Her name is Ashleigh.
